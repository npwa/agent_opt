### The Yamnaya (Pit Grave) Culture: Core Details

**Yamnaya culture** (also known as **Yamna**, **Pit Grave culture**, or **Ochre Grave culture**) was a late Copper Age to early Bronze Age archaeological culture of the Pontic–Caspian steppe that dates to approximately **3300–2600 BC**. It was discovered by Vasily Gorodtsov following excavations near the Donets River in 1901–1903.

- **Etymology**: *Yamnaya* is a Russian adjective meaning 'related to pits' (from *yama*, яма), reflecting its characteristic burial tradition.
- **Burial practice**: The dead were buried in tumuli (kurgans) containing simple pit chambers. Bodies were placed in a supine position with bent knees and covered in ochre.[^yamnaya1]
- **Geography**: Stretching between the Southern Bug, Dniester, and Ural rivers—the Pontic–Caspian steppe.[^yamnaya2]
- **Economy**: Nomadic or semi-nomadic pastoralism, animal husbandry, fishing, foraging, and ceramic manufacturing. They used two-wheeled carts and four-wheeled wagons (oxen-drawn) and evidence of horse riding.[^yamnaya3]

**Genetic makeup of Yamnaya**: Recent higher-resolution genetic modeling shows the Yamnaya derived approximately **80% of their ancestry from a Caucasus–Lower Volga (CLV) population**, rather than being a simple 50/50 mixture. The CLV population itself was roughly ~67% CHG and ~32% EHG.[^yamnaya4]

### Ancient DNA and Indo-European Migrations: Key Genetic Findings

The Yamnaya people's genetic profile is characterized by a distinctive composition that links ancient steppes to wide geographic expansion. Multiple foundational studies are cited in the Wikipedia literature on Yamnaya culture:[^yamnaya5]

#### 1. The EHG + CHG Admixture Model (Jones et al. 2015; Haak et al. 2015)

The initial model proposed by Jones et al. (2015) and Haak et al. (2015) found that autosomal tests indicate Yamnaya resulted from admixture between:
- **Eastern Hunter-Gatherers (EHG)**: From Eastern Europe, genetically close to the Mal'ta–Buret' culture or related Siberian populations.
- **Caucasus Hunter-Gatherers (CHG)**: Arriving from the Caucasus region.[^yamnaya6]

Each contributed roughly **half** of Yamnaya DNA—an admixture termed **"Western Steppe Herder" (WSH) ancestry**. This model is widely cited in subsequent literature.[^yamnaya7]

#### 2. Refined CHG Ancestry via Maykop (source not verified)

A key finding from the Lazaridis et al. (2016) study was that **CHG ancestry in Yamnaya derives from a population directly related to CHG individuals buried in the later Maykop culture of western Transcaucasia (~3700 BC)**, rather than Iranian CHG sources.[^yamnaya8]

This resolved an earlier puzzle: why "Iranian farmer-related" admixture was absent from Yamnaya even though their ancestry closely resembled CHG.[^yamnaya9]

#### 3. Steppe Genetic Cline and Anatolian Farmer-Related Admixture (source not verified)

Lazaridis, Patterson, Anthony, and Vyazovitskaya (2025) demonstrated the Yamnaya's genetic composition as involving:
- ~80% Caucasus–Lower Volga (CLV) ancestry and
- ~20% Anatolian farmer-related (Anatolia_N) admixture, **not** European Early European Farmers (EEFs).[^yamnaya10]

Crucially, the **Anatolian component in Yamnaya came from Caucasus Neolithic populations**, not from Anatolia-dervived European farmers — a distinction clarified by Nikitin, Lazaridis, Patterson, and Ivanova (2025).^ [^yamnaya11]

#### 4. Broad Indo-European Migration Impact

The spread of Steppe ancestry through major migrations correlates with the expansion of Indo-European languages:[^ie_mig1]

- **Corded Ware culture** (3000–2400 BC): Derived large parts of ancestry from Yamnaya.[^yamnaya12]
- **Bell Beaker culture** (2900–1800 BC): Also inherited significant Steppe-related ancestry.[^yamnaya13]
- **Sintashta and Andronovo cultures**: Carried Steppe ancestry, connected with Indo-Iranian language expansion.
- **Afanasievo culture** (Siberia, ~3500–2500 BC): Genetically indistinguishable from Yamnaya.[^yamnaya14]

### Key Genetic Haplogroups and Phenotypic Traits

- **Y-DNA**: **Haplogroup R1b-Z2103** (a subclade of R1b-L23) is the most common in Yamnaya samples[^yamnaya15]. Minority presence of J2 and I2 also noted.
- **mtDNA haplogroups**: U, T, and others linked to Caucasus hunter-gatherers and Early European Farmers.[^yamnaya16] Notably, a small but significant number from Northern Ukraine carried East Asian mtDNA haplogroup C4.[^yamnaya17]
- **Blond hair mutation**: Some Yamnaya individuals likely carried the KITLG gene mutation for blond hair, possibly sourced from Ancient North Eurasian (ANE) Afontova Gora populations—several Steppe-descended individuals inherited this.[^yamnaya18]
- **Height**: A 2015 study found Yamnaya showed the highest ever calculated genetic selection for height among ancient populations tested.[^yamnaya19]
