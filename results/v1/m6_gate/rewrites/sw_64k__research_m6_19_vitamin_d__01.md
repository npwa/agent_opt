Based on the VITAL trial (VITamin D and OmegA-3 TriaL), large randomized controlled trial evidence **does not support** vitamin D supplementation for preventing cancer, cardiovascular disease, or fractures in the general population of generally healthy adults. Below is a detailed breakdown by outcome with specific citations.

---

## Trial Design

The VITAL trial was a nationwide, double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-by-two factorial randomized trial of 25,871 U.S. adults — men aged ≥50 and women aged ≥55. Participants were **not selected on the basis of vitamin D deficiency, low bone mass, or osteoporosis**, making the findings directly relevant to general-population primary prevention. (Mean age was 67.1 years at enrollment; 20.2% of participants were Black.) The trial arm tested vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) at 2,000 IU/day against placebo, with a median follow-up of 5.3 years.

- **Trial protocol**: Manson JE, Bassuk SS, Lee IM, Cook NR, Albert MA, et al. [The VITamin D and OmegA-3 TriaL (VITAL): rationale and design of a large randomized controlled trial of vitamin D and marine omega-3 fatty acid supplements for the primary prevention of cancer and cardiovascular disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21986389/). *Contemp Clin Trials*. 2012;33(1):159–171. DOI: 10.1016/j.cct.2011.09.009. PMID: 21986389 (PMC3253961).
- **ClinicalTrials.gov**: NCT01169259 (vitamin D component); NCT01704157, NCT01704859 (ancillary sub-cohorts).

---

## Cancer Prevention — No Benefit Demonstrated

The vitamin D arm did **not** significantly reduce the risk of invasive cancer compared with placebo.

- **Total invasive cancer**: Diagnosed in 793 participants on vitamin D vs. 824 on placebo; hazard ratio (HR) 0.96; 95% CI, 0.88–1.06; *P* = 0.47.
- **Death from cancer**: HR 0.83; 95% CI, 0.67–1.02 (*not statistically significant*).
- **Site-specific cancers** — breast cancer: HR 1.02 (95% CI, 0.79–1.31); prostate cancer: HR 0.88 (95% CI, 0.72–1.07); colorectal cancer: HR 1.09 (95% CI, 0.73–1.62).
- **Death from any cause**: HR 0.99 (95% CI, 0.87–1.12).

**Conclusion**: Daily supplemental vitamin D3 at a dose of 2,000 IU did not result in a lower incidence of invasive cancer than placebo.

**Citation**: Manson JE, Cook NR, Lee IM, Christen W, Bassuk SS, Mora S, et al.; VITAL Research Group. [Vitamin D Supplements and Prevention of Cancer and Cardiovascular Disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415629/). *N Engl J Med*. 2019;380(1):33–44. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1809944. PMID: 30415629 (PMC6425757).

---

## Cardiovascular Disease Prevention — No Benefit Demonstrated

The vitamin D arm also did **not** significantly reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events compared with placebo.

- **Major cardiovascular event composite** (myocardial infarction, stroke, or death from cardiovascular causes): Occurred in 396 participants on vitamin D vs. 409 on placebo; HR 0.97; 95% CI, 0.85–1.12; *P* = 0.69.
- **Expanded composite** (major CVD events plus coronary revascularization): HR 0.96 (95% CI, 0.86–1.08).
- Myocardial infarction alone: HR 0.96 (95% CI, 0.78–1.19).
- Stroke alone: HR 0.95 (95% CI, 0.76–1.20).
- Death from cardiovascular causes: HR 1.11 (95% CI, 0.88–1.40).

**Conclusion**: Supplementation with vitamin D3 did not result in a lower incidence of major cardiovascular events than placebo.

**Citation**: Manson JE, Cook NR, Lee IM, Christen W, Bassuk SS, Mora S, et al.; VITAL Research Group. [Vitamin D Supplements and Prevention of Cancer and Cardiovascular Disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30415629/). *N Engl J Med*. 2019;380(1):33–44. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1809944. PMID: 30415629 (PMC6425757).

---

## Fracture Prevention — No Benefit Demonstrated

In an ancillary VITAL bone health sub-cohort of 25,871 participants (the full trial population), supplementation with vitamin D3 also did **not** reduce incident fractures.

- **Total fractures**: Confirmed in 1,991 incidents among 1,551 participants over a median follow-up of 5.3 years. No significant effect of vitamin D on total fractures: HR 0.98 (95% CI, 0.89–1.08; *P* = 0.70).
- **Nonvertebral fractures**: HR 0.97 (95% CI, 0.87–1.07; *P* = 0.50).
- **Hip fractures**: HR 1.01 (95% CI, 0.70–1.47; *P* = 0.96).
- No modification of the treatment effect was found according to baseline characteristics, including age, sex, race or ethnic group, BMI, or serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels.

**Conclusion**: Vitamin D3 supplementation did not result in a significantly lower risk of fractures than placebo among generally healthy midlife and older adults who were not selected for vitamin D deficiency, low bone mass, or osteoporosis (source not verified).

---

## Falls Prevention — No Benefit Demonstrated

Separately, vitamin D supplementation did not reduce fall risk either in the VITAL trial population.

- **Two or more falls**: 9.8% on vitamin D vs. 9.4% on placebo; odds ratio (OR) = 0.97 (95% CI, 0.90–1.05; *P* = 0.50).
- Falls resulting in a doctor visit: OR 1.03 (95% CI, 0.94–1.13; *P* = 0.46).
- Falls resulting in a hospital visit: OR 1.04 (95% CI, 0.90–1.19; *P* = 0.61).
- Results did **not** differ between those with baseline 25(OH)D <50 nmol/L versus ≥50 nmol/L.

**Citation**: LeBoff MS, Murata EM, Cook NR, Cawchoon PM, Chou SH, et al. [VITamin D and OmegA-3 TriaL (VITAL): Effects of Vitamin D Supplements on Risk of Falls in the US Population](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32492153/). *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*. 2020;105(9):2929–2938. DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgaa311. PMID: 32492153 (PMC7365686).

---

## Overall Summary

The VITAL trial — one of the largest randomized controlled trials ever conducted on vitamin D supplementation in a general-population setting — found **no statistically significant benefit** of daily 2,000 IU/day vitamin D3 compared with placebo for the prevention of:

- Invasive cancer (HR 0.96; 95% CI, 0.88–1.06)
- Major cardiovascular events (HR 0.97; 95% CI, 0.85–1.12)
- Incident fractures of any type (HR 0.98; 95% CI, 0.89–1.08)
- Falls leading to medical visits or hospitalizations

These null findings held across predefined subgroups and in individuals with baseline serum 25(OH)D as low as <50 nmol/L. The evidence from VITAL does not support routine vitamin D supplementation for primary prevention of cancer, cardiovascular disease, fractures, or falls in the general population of generally healthy adults who are not selected for deficiency or osteoporosis.
