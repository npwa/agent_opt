### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has fundamentally transformed the study of exoplanet atmospheres, enabling direct chemical probes of habitability-relevant worlds for the first time. This report presents a comparative analysis of two emblematic targets: **TRAPPIST-1e**, an Earth-sized rocky planet orbiting within the habitable zone (HZ) of an ultracool M8V dwarf star; and **K2-18b**, a sub-Neptune/hycean-type world residing in the HZ of a brighter M3V star. While JWST's NIRSpec observations of TRAPPIST-1e have yielded inconclusive but constraining results — ruling out hydrogen-dominated atmospheres while leaving open the possibility of bare rock, cloudy skies, or nitrogen-rich compositions — K2-18b has shown tantalizing spectroscopic evidence for carbon-bearing molecules including methane (CH₄) and carbon dioxide (CO₂), with a tentative ~3$\sigma$ signal attributed to dimethyl sulfide (DMS). This report synthesizes all published JWST findings through September 2025, contextualizes them within stellar-environment considerations, presents comparative physical parameter tables, and discusses the scientific pathway forward.
